PAWLY Incrementality Lab

Five questions a small advertiser asks about its ads, each answered with the study that fits it: a geo experiment designed for power, a causal-impact readout of a pause, a check of what AI auto-bidding really added, a media mix model calibrated with the experiments, and a cross-platform check of platform and GA4 credit. Every number is graded against the hidden truth of the simulated world.

PAWLY was chosen as a typical fast-growing small D2C advertiser whose profit depends on repeat purchases; the methods apply to any e-commerce advertiser.

Known limits

  • One simulated world. The geo test was re-run on 60 noise draws; the other chapters were checked with placebo windows, not repeated worlds.
  • The control series is cleaner than real ones. The category-search index that drives the causal-impact model follows true demand with 5% noise. Real covariates (search trends, competitor sales) are noisier and can be touched by the campaign, which widens intervals and can bias them.
  • TBR with A/A calibration instead of Trimmed Match. TBR reads the go-dark test from group totals, as in GeoX; its interval was fixed with historical A/A tests. Trimmed Match, the pair-level estimator that trims outlier pairs automatically, would be the more robust choice when pairs differ a lot; here the pair diagnostic and an advertiser check do that trimming by hand.
  • GA4 is simulated at channel level, from the platforms' own claims. The GA4 export applies stated rules (site share of sales, consent loss, a multiplier on each platform's claim) rather than user paths, so the direction of its mix partly follows the platform reports by construction. It shows how to read GA4 data, not an independent second proof; real GA4 paths would spread credit differently.
  • Korea has few usable geo units. 40 regions of comparable size is generous: matched pairs in Korea would be built from city and county clusters (si-gun-gu), with Seoul and Gyeonggi hard to split, and sales by region need shipping addresses. Where pairs cannot be matched, the playbook's fallback is a user-level conversion lift study.
  • The world is mine. The methods were tested against problems I chose; the log below records where they failed a check, including results that came out differently from the plan.
